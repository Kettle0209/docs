A payout is your profit share, paid to your bank in INR, once you hold a trading account. This page covers your share, when you can request a payout, and how the funds reach your account.

## Your profit share

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Profit share | Up to 80% of profit (78% at the two largest account sizes) |
| Paid in | INR, to your Indian bank account |
| Processing time | Typically under 24 hours to payout |
| First payout | After 5 active trading days |
| After that | On demand — request whenever you have profit available |
| Minimum payout | None |

Your profit is calculated in USD against your account size, and Xflow converts it to INR at the rate at the time of each payout. There is no fixed payout window, no monthly cycle, and no limit on how often you withdraw. Every payout is recorded in your account history.

Before your first withdrawal, you complete a quick identity check (KYC). This protects your account.

<Note>
  Wield is a simulated environment that runs on live market data. No real assets are bought, sold, or held, and no order reaches an exchange — and none of your own money is in it. Payouts are your profit share from a trading account, which you receive only after you pass an evaluation.
</Note>

## How funds reach your bank

Payouts settle to your Indian bank account in INR through Xflow (Xflow Payments India Pvt. Ltd.), a cross-border payment partner authorised by the Reserve Bank of India.

<Steps>
  <Step title="You request a payout">
    From your available profit, after the first-payout activity period.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Payment is initiated through Xflow">
    The payment is made from Wield — operated by Vipas Ventures MB — to you, an India-resident provider of analysis, under a services purpose code. Your USD profit is converted to INR at the prevailing rate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="INR settles to your account">
    The amount is credited to your bank account in INR, against your PAN. An electronic Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (eFIRA) is issued in your name for each payment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  **What an eFIRA is**

  An eFIRA is an official bank record. It shows that your INR came from a known company outside India, as payment for a service — the same proof IT exporters and freelancers get when they invoice clients abroad.

  Each one names who sent the money, how much, the date, and you as the person who received it.

  It does not stop a bank from asking about a transfer — nothing does. But every payment you receive has a clear sender, a regulated payment route, and a record behind it.
</Info>

## How payouts are taxed

Your payout comes with an eFIRA in your name — an official bank record showing it as a service payment from a company outside India. On that basis it is treated as professional-services income, not a crypto gain — a different category from the flat 30% on crypto gains, and for many people a lower one.

How this works for you depends on your situation, and your CA can confirm it. Wield gives you the documents; it does not give tax advice.
