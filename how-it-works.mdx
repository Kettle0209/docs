Wield gives skilled traders a trading account without putting up their own capital. You prove your trading in an evaluation, earn a trading account, and keep a share of the profit you make — paid to your bank in INR.

Here is the whole path.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Take an evaluation">
    Choose an account size and pay a one-time fee. You then trade to reach a profit target while staying within the risk limits. You can reach the target in a single trade — there is no rule about how many days it takes. [See Evaluations](/evaluations).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Earn a trading account">
    Pass the evaluation and you earn a trading account at the size you chose. You do not deposit any money of your own. There is no longer a profit target — you trade within the same risk limits and keep your profit share. [See Trading Accounts](/trading-accounts).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get paid in INR">
    Withdraw your profit share on demand — as often as you like, including more than once a day. Your profit is converted to INR and sent to your bank through an RBI-authorised partner, with documentation issued in your name. [See Payouts](/payouts).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trade more, and larger">
    You can take more than one evaluation and hold several accounts at the same time, in different sizes. You can also be offered a larger size over time as you keep trading well. [See Scaling](/scaling).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you are paying for

The one-time fee buys the evaluation — where you prove your trading and earn a trading account. It does not buy a trading account directly, and passing is not guaranteed. The evaluation is a real test of skill, and many people do not pass it.

<Note>
  You trade in a simulated environment that runs on live market data. No real assets are bought or sold, and no order reaches an exchange. None of your own money is ever in the account. What is real is the profit share paid to you in INR for the analysis you provide.
</Note>

## The two stages, side by side

The evaluation and the trading account use the same risk limits, but they are different stages. The main difference is the profit target.

|  | Evaluation | Trading account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profit target | Yes — reach it to pass | None |
| Maximum loss | Applies | Applies |
| Maximum daily loss | Applies | Applies |
| You pay | A one-time fee | Nothing |
| You earn | A trading account | Your profit share, in INR |
