Pass an evaluation to earn a Wield trading account. You do not need to fund the account yourself. You trade freely — without consistency rules, profit caps, or limits on your trading style.

From that point there is no profit target to hit. You keep your profit share on the profit you make, and you trade within institutional risk parameters: a maximum loss and a maximum daily loss. This page explains those limits, what is not restricted, and what ends an account.

## Your risk limits

A trading account has two limits: a **maximum loss** and a **maximum daily loss**. Both are measured against your account size and are fixed when the account starts.

| Account size | Max loss (account ends below) | Max daily loss (in one day) | Your profit share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \$10,000 | 10% — floor at \$9,000 | 5% — \$500 | 80% |
| \$25,000 | 10% — floor at \$22,500 | 5% — \$1,250 | 80% |
| \$50,000 | 10% — floor at \$45,000 | 5% — \$2,500 | 80% |
| \$100,000 | 10% — floor at \$90,000 | 5% — \$5,000 | 78% |
| \$200,000 | 10% — floor at \$180,000 | 5% — \$10,000 | 78% |

Account size is a reference figure your account is measured against. It is not money you deposit or hold. Your profit share, when you withdraw it, is paid as real INR — see [Payouts](/payouts).

### Maximum loss

This is the lowest your account value can go. On a \$50,000 account, the floor is \$45,000. If your account value falls to that floor, the account ends.

The floor is fixed at the start and does not move up as you make profit. So as your account grows, the distance to the floor grows with it. A \$50,000 account that reaches \$58,000 still has its floor at \$45,000 — your buffer is now larger, not reset higher.

<Note>
  This is a fixed floor, not a trailing one. Many firms move the floor up behind your profits, so a profitable account can still be closed on a small pullback. Wield does not do this. The floor stays where it started.
</Note>

### Maximum daily loss

This is the most your account can fall within one day. The day runs in Indian Standard Time (IST), from 00:00 to 23:59. On a \$50,000 account, the daily limit is \$2,500. If your account falls by that much within a single IST day, the account ends. The limit resets at the start of each new IST day.

## What is not restricted

Your account ends only for the reasons in the next section. Outside of those, how you trade is your own:

- **No consistency rule.** Your profit can come from a single trade or a single day. None of it is capped or set aside because of how it was earned.
- **No time limit.** There is no deadline and no minimum number of days, beyond the activity needed for your first payout.
- **No restriction on news or weekends.** You can trade around news events and on weekends.

These rules do not change the [Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/acceptable-use), which still applies.

## What ends an account

An account ends in any of these cases:

<Steps>
  <Step title="You reach the maximum loss">
    Your account value falls to or below its fixed floor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You reach the maximum daily loss">
    Your account falls by the daily limit within one IST day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You break the Acceptable Use Policy">
    For example, sharing an account or coordinating across accounts. See the [Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/acceptable-use).
  </Step>
  <Step title="You choose to stop">
    You can end your account yourself at any time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Passing an evaluation and trading well afterwards are not the same thing. Many traders who pass an evaluation later reach a limit on their trading account. Each account is independent, and how you traded before does not decide how you will trade next.

## Getting back after a breach

If your account ends on a breach, you can start again at the same size within 30 days, at half the usual evaluation fee. The point is practical: one bad day should not mean paying full price to return to a level your trading has already reached. It is a return path, not a subscription — each re-entry is a single evaluation like any other.
